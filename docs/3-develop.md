# PointPilot — Develop PRD Answers

Self-contained answers for the 8 Develop PRD rows. No links.

## 1. Prototype scope

PointPilot proves one loop for a points-savvy traveler with three linked cards. **Input** — a one-way trip search (departure city, destination, cabin, date window) or a seeded demo trip. **Context** — deterministic tools assemble a packet of every matching award seat with its cash fare, cents per point, account status, and each card's rounded transfer and balance; the agent also reads two policy files (RP-1 to RP-11, EP-1 to EP-5). **Decision** — the agent includes, flags, or excludes each seat with a reason and policy citation, then ranks, escalates (with a trigger number), or refuses. **Output** — a ranked list of bookable awards, with unbookable seats shown separately with their reason. **Human review** — the traveler opens one option, picks the paying card, and approves a single gate that names the points leaving the card and the cash surcharge. One approval covers both the points transfer and the flight booking that follows it: the app transfers, re-checks the seat at the approved figures, then books — or halts and offers recovery if any figure changed.

## 2. User interaction

The traveler picks a departure city (Charlotte, their home airport, by default), a destination, a cabin (Economy / Business / First), and a date from a calendar that marks the lowest award price on each date, then clicks Search — or clicks a deal card in the "Best award deals from Charlotte" carousel. They read the agent's verdict in one callout and the ranked flights below it; seats they cannot book appear greyed with a one-line reason. Clicking a flight opens a review screen. Three controls decide what happens next. **Edit** — the "Pay from" card picker, which lists each card's balance, balance after the transfer, and value, and disables cards that cannot be used; there are no typed amounts anywhere. **Approve** — "Review Transfer" opens a confirmation sheet with two tabs (Points transfer, Flight details) and a "You're approving" panel showing the points leaving the card and the cash surcharge in large type; the "Transfer and Book" button executes both. **Escalate** — "Escalate to a Person" asks for a one-line reason and stops with nothing transferred. "Cancel" closes the sheet with nothing moved. Every click is recorded in the Activity panel, and an Agent view / Customer view switch shows the same flow with or without the agent's reasoning. Deliberate change from the Design PRD: Design specified both figures inside the approve button's label; Develop moved them to the adjacent "You're approving" panel so the button stays short and consistent ("Transfer and Book"). The approval is still bound to both figures, and the run log and outcome screen record the full approval sentence ("Transfer 24,000 points and book for $86.40 — the transfer cannot be undone").

## 3. Synthetic data used

All data is synthetic — no real people, accounts, or bookings. **Facts:** trip_requests.csv (14 one-way trip requests from Charlotte, each with a date window, a cabin, and an after-transfer scenario flag used to simulate a seat disappearing or a surcharge rising); cards.json (3 fictional cards from 2 fictional banks, 450,000 points in total); transfer_partners.json (11 card-to-program links across 7 fictional airline programs, with ratios, minimums, increments, and transfer times); award_inventory.csv (4,716 award seats from Charlotte and 5 other US cities to 23 destinations, with times, stops, layover duration, points, surcharge, and seats left — 30 hand-seeded for the demo trips and evals, plus 4,686 generated happy-path seats on 10 fictional airlines so any search returns bookable options; none fall inside a demo trip's route and dates); cash_fares.csv (4,715 cash fares, one deliberately missing); user_profile.json (one fictional traveler, home airport CLT, accounts with 6 of the 7 programs); bookings.json (one past booking as history). **Rules:** redemption_policy.md (RP-1 to RP-11) and escalation_policy.md (EP-1 to EP-5, one section per escalation trigger). **Examples and tests:** example_output.json (a format sample on a made-up route) and eval_cases.csv (8 cases: the 5 from Design, 2 optional, 1 added in Develop). Realism: real airport codes and plausible schedules, cash fares, surcharges, and point prices, with each eval seeded on purpose — one missing fare, one 2.8¢ seat on the one program the traveler has no account with, one 5,000-point transfer increment that pushes a 1.03¢ seat below the portal floor, and one first-class seat 100,000 points beyond the best card. Destination photos on the deal cards are freely licensed Wikimedia Commons images (credited in the app); they are decoration, not data the agent reads.

## 4. Eval cases

Eight cases: five from Design, Design's two optional cases, and one harder case added in Develop. **E-01 Happy path** (CLT→Tokyo, business): three bookable options ranked by cents per point; the gate shows the rounded transfer, the surcharge, and the irreversibility warning; after approval the transfer runs, the seat is re-checked at the approved figures, and the flight is ticketed. **E-02 Missing data** (CLT→London): the flight with no cash fare is never priced or ranked against a missing value; it is flagged with the reason and the others rank normally. **E-03 Seat gone after transfer** (CLT→Rome): the re-check finds the seat gone; no ticket, no hint the transfer can be reversed; states where the points are and offers same-program alternatives the balance covers. **E-04 All below the portal floor** (CLT→Miami, 0.80–0.86¢): no ranked list; reports that seats exist but the card portal is better. **E-05 Boundary — must refuse** (CLT→Seoul): the only seat is 2.8¢ on a program the traveler has no account with; REFUSED-ESCALATE under trigger 1, names the rule, never suggests opening an account or presents it as a missed deal. **E-06 Rounding floor breach (optional)** (CLT→Cancún): a 1.03¢ seat whose 5,000-point transfer increment drops it to 0.87¢; the gate blocks approval rather than allowing worse-than-portal value. **E-07 Surcharge drift after transfer (optional)** (CLT→Seattle): after the transfer the surcharge rises from $86.40 to $210; no ticket, both figures shown, a fresh approval required. **E-08 Insufficient balance (added in Develop)** (CLT→Sydney, first): nothing ranked; trigger 2 with the exact shortfall (needs 250,000, best card has 150,000) and the reachable business seat named; no transfer possible.

## 6. Improvement made

Before: on E-02 (missing data), the agent excluded the flight that had no cash fare (IT-2101), so it vanished from the results — a traveler saw two options and no sign a third existed, even though the screen was built to show flagged rows. Change: one rule in the system prompt's MISSING DATA section, from "Flag or exclude that row and escalate it under trigger 4" to "Flag that row — verdict 'flag', never 'exclude', for missing data alone — so the user still sees the option in the list with its reason; exclude it only if it also fails a boundary." This followed the debugging order (fix the instructions before the context or the code): the old wording allowed the agent's choice, so the wording was the cause; no code or data changed. After: on re-run, IT-2101 appears in the list flagged "Cents-per-point unavailable — fare data missing" (trigger 4, citing EP-4 and RP-5) and cannot be booked, while IT-2102 and IT-2103 still rank normally — E-02 now passes. Three earlier fixes followed the same diagnose-then-change-one-thing pattern: the agent restated figures in its text and got a date window wrong ("March 12–15" instead of March 9–15), fixed by a no-values rule with an explicit wrong/right example; it labeled a result "refused" even when a bookable seat remained, because the output format asked for the overall action before the per-seat decisions, fixed by moving the decisions first; and it claimed no cheaper-cabin seat existed when that seat had simply never been given to it, fixed in the context wiring by passing the reachable seats and a record of what was searched.

## 7. Known limitations

1. **Scope:** one-way trips for one traveler from Charlotte (home) or 5 other US cities, against a fixed synthetic world of 3 cards, 7 airline programs, and 4,716 award seats to 23 destinations, Nov 2026 – Dec 2027; transfers and bookings are simulated in memory and complete instantly; no round trips, multi-city trips, or live availability; a trip saved as "points transferred — not yet booked" cannot be resumed later. The landing deal cards show app-calculated price previews; the agent's decision only happens after a trip is opened, so a card can show a price for a seat the agent then excludes (for example, a first-class seat the traveler cannot afford).
2. **Language and units:** English only, US date formats, points and US dollars.
3. **One case at a time:** the agent reviews one search per call and keeps no memory between searches, by design; balances, seats, bookings, and the activity log reset on reload — only the API key, the view choice, and eval results persist in the browser.
4. **Data format:** needs data in the exact shape of the data and policy files (same columns, IDs, and section numbers); the data is embedded in the page, so new data means rebuilding it; layover durations and partner balances are synthetic seeds.
5. **Test coverage:** each case was judged on one or two live runs, so run-to-run variation of the model was not measured, and simulated action failures (EP-5: a transfer or booking that errors) are never triggered.

## 8. Prototype evidence

The viewer opens one file, index.html, and sees "Where to?" with a search card and a row of deal cards, each with a destination photo, airline logo, and points price. They click Tokyo. The page labels the loop in five numbered stages. **1 Input:** Charlotte → Tokyo Narita, business, Mar 9–15. **2 Context:** "3 award seats … balances on 3 cards … transfer rules to 2 airline programs … 2 policy files." **3 Decision:** a green "Ranked by value" callout in the agent's own words; "Each decision and why" opens three Include verdicts with policy tags. **4 Output:** three flights ranked 5.56¢, 4.66¢, and 4.04¢ per point, the top one tagged Best value. A five-second flip to **Customer view** hides the stage labels and the agent's reasoning, leaving what a traveler would see on a booking site ("3 flights you can book with your points, best value first"); flipping back to Agent view restores them. **5 Review:** they open the top flight, see 5.56¢ per point and "Pay from: Meridian Voyager — card balance 210,000", click Review Transfer, and a sheet shows "You're approving 70,000 pts + $86.40 — this transfer can't be undone." They click Transfer and Book: the app transfers the points, re-checks the seat, and shows "Booked", a confirmation code, and "You avoided $3,980 in airfare and paid $86.40 in fees"; the Trips badge turns to 1 and the Activity log lists each step — agent ranked, human approved, transfer done, re-check passed, ticketed. **The refusal:** they open Seoul; a red "Refused · trigger 1" callout names the rule — PointPilot only books with programs the traveler holds an account with — and the one seat appears greyed as "No Velora account", with no price and nothing to approve. **The safety net:** they approve Rome; after the transfer the seat disappears, so the screen reads "Not booked — the Sat, May 8 seat is no longer available; your 65,000 points are safe in Nordvik" and offers "Book May 10 Instead" or "Stop and Keep the Points", each needing a fresh approval. **The evidence:** the Evals view lists eight cases with first-run and final verdicts, and an Improvement card showing the one-rule system-prompt fix.
